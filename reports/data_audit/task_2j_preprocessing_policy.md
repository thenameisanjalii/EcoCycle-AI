## 2. Locked Preprocessing Leakage Rules

Every transformation, imputer, scaler, and pipeline component must conform to the following twelve inviolable rules:

1. **Battery-Grouped Split:** Splitting must strictly occur along the `battery_id` boundary (70% Train / 15% Validation / 15% Test, Seed 42).
2. **Metadata Key Only:** `battery_id` is grouping metadata only and must never enter the feature matrix $X$.
3. **Identifier Prohibition:** `battery_id` must never be an input feature to prevent memorization leakage.
4. **SOH Target Lock:** `SOH` is the target variable for the SOH regression task and must never enter $X_{SOH}$.
5. **RUL Target Lock:** `RUL` is the target variable for the RUL regression task and must never enter $X_{RUL}$.
6. **Primary Model Exclusion of QDischarge:** `QDischarge` is strictly excluded from the primary SOH model feature set due to direct target leakage ($r = 0.9989$).
7. **Deployment Independence:** Primary deployment models must not depend on `QDischarge`, as field discharge capacity is unknown prior to full cycle completion.
8. **Ground-Truth SOH Cascade Prohibition:** Ground-truth `SOH` must never be used by the production RUL model. The production RUL model must consume predicted SOH ($\widehat{	ext{SOH}}$) generated by the primary SOH model.
9. **RUL Strict Target Status:** `RUL` must never be an input feature under any operating mode.
10. **Train-Only Parameter Fitting:** All preprocessing statistics, scalers, quantile bounds, and imputer parameters must be fitted **strictly on training partition batteries**.
11. **Strict Causal Transformation:** Validation and test partitions must only be transformed using objects fitted on the training partition.
12. **Causal Time-Series Directionality:** No backward-looking transformations or future cycle information ($t+k$) may be used to impute or construct features for cycle $t$.

## 3. Anomaly-by-Anomaly Policy Specification

### Issue 1: Zero Internal Resistance (`IR == 0.0`)

#### 1. Problem
A total of **4,922 records (4.219% of the dataset)** contain an internal resistance value of exactly `0.0 Ohm`. In physical electrochemical cells, internal resistance cannot be zero; an impedance of zero is physically impossible in operating lithium-ion cells.

#### 2. Evidence
- **Systemic Occurrence (Batteries 87–93):** Exactly 7 batteries (`87, 88, 89, 90, 91, 92, 93`) account for **4,903 of the zero records (99.61% of all zeros)**. For all 7 batteries, 100% of their recorded cycles have `IR == 0.0` (cycle counts: 458, 463, 497, 1061, 874, 1027, and 523). In these batteries, the cycling hardware or data acquisition protocol had the high-frequency impedance measurement step disabled. However, all other telemetry fields (`QDischarge`, `QCharge`, `Tavg`, `Tmax`, `Tmin`, `chargetime`, `cycle`, `SOH`) are completely valid, exhibiting normal electrochemical degradation patterns (SOH degrading from 100% down to ~75%).
- **Isolated Occurrence (16 Batteries):** Exactly 16 batteries (`1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11, 12, 17, 21, 22, 24, 41`) contain 1 or 2 isolated zero records (19 records total, 0.39% of all zeros). These represent transient sensor dropouts where preceding and succeeding cycles exhibit normal IR (~0.015 to 0.020 Ohm).

#### 3. Decision
- **ADD MISSINGNESS INDICATOR** + **IMPUTE**
  1. Add a binary feature: `IR_is_zero` $\in \{0, 1\}$.
  2. For **isolated zeros** (where prior cycles exist within the same battery): apply strictly **causal forward-fill** ($IR_t = IR_{t-1}$).
  3. For **systemic zero-IR batteries** (where the entire battery history is zero): impute $IR$ with the **median non-zero IR computed strictly on the Training partition** ($IR_{impute} = 	ext{Median}(IR_{	ext{train}, IR > 0}) pprox 0.01614	ext{ }\Omega$).
  4. **DO NOT DELETE ROWS.** Retain all 4,922 cycles.

#### 4. Reason
Dropping 4,922 cycles (including 7 complete battery lifecycles) would discard critical degradation trajectories, reducing training volume and creating an artificial data distribution. The combination of median imputation and an explicit binary missingness indicator (`IR_is_zero`) preserves the full dataset while equipping tree-based models (e.g., LightGBM, XGBoost) and neural architectures to learn separate partition splits or bias adjustments for cells without active impedance telemetry.

#### 5. Leakage Risk
- **HIGH** if the median is computed across the entire dataset (incorporating Validation/Test batteries).
- **ZERO** when the median is computed strictly from non-zero records in the **Training partition** and applied downstream to Validation/Test sets.
- **ZERO** for causal forward-fill because it references only historical cycles ($t-1$), avoiding future temporal leakage.

#### 6. Deployment Implication
In real-world battery management systems (BMS), impedance measurement is frequently omitted to save cost or power, or may experience intermittent communication dropouts. The proposed policy allows the production model to gracefully handle incoming telemetry whether the BMS provides active IR measurements or not.

### Issue 2: Battery 85 Temperature Sensor Saturation (`Tmin = -270 °C`, `Tmax = 400 °C`)

#### 1. Problem
All 99 cycles of **Battery 85** exhibit fixed extreme values for minimum and maximum temperature: `Tmin = -270.0 °C` and `Tmax = 400.0 °C`. In addition, 8 cycles have negative average temperatures (`Tavg` between -8.66 °C and -0.12 °C).

#### 2. Evidence
- In physical cryogenics, -270.0 °C corresponds to 3.15 K, which is close to absolute zero and physically impossible in an ambient laboratory battery testing facility.
- 400.0 °C is an extreme thermal runaway temperature; if an 18650 cell reached 400 °C, catastrophic thermal destruction would occur.
- However, Battery 85 shows completely healthy, normal electrochemical behavior: `QDischarge` is normal (mean 1.084 Ah), `QCharge` is normal (mean 1.081 Ah), `IR` is normal (mean 0.0165 Ohm), and `SOH` declines smoothly from 100.09% to 98.96%.
- Furthermore, $T_{\min} \le T_{	ext{avg}} \le T_{\max}$ holds mathematically for all 99 cycles.
- This evidence confirms an **open-circuit thermocouple fault with ADC saturation limits** (-270 °C lower hardware limit, 400 °C upper hardware limit).
- In Task 2I's battery split, **Battery 85 is assigned to the Training partition**.

#### 3. Decision
- **ADD MISSINGNESS INDICATOR** + **IMPUTE**
  1. Add a binary feature: `temp_sensor_fault` $\in \{0, 1\}$.
  2. For Battery 85, set `temp_sensor_fault = 1` (0 for normal batteries).
  3. Impute `Tmin` and `Tmax` using the median temperature offsets derived strictly from healthy training batteries:
     - $T_{\min} = T_{	ext{avg}} - \Delta T_{\min,	ext{train}}$ where $\Delta T_{\min,	ext{train}} = 	ext{Median}(T_{	ext{avg}} - T_{\min})_{	ext{train, healthy}}$
     - $T_{\max} = T_{	ext{avg}} + \Delta T_{\max,	ext{train}}$ where $\Delta T_{\max,	ext{train}} = 	ext{Median}(T_{\max} - T_{	ext{avg}})_{	ext{train, healthy}}$
  4. For the 8 negative `Tavg` cycles in Battery 85, impute `Tavg` using causal interpolation from adjacent valid cycles within Battery 85 or replace with the training cohort median ambient temperature (~33.5 °C).
  5. **DO NOT DELETE BATTERY 85.** Retain all 99 cycles.

#### 4. Reason
Because Battery 85 resides in the Training partition, feeding raw values of -270 °C and 400 °C would severely distort standard scalers (e.g., StandardScaler would yield massive variances: std of Tmax is 10.84 °C solely due to these 99 rows). Robust imputation restores physically sensible values while the indicator flag ensures model transparency.

#### 5. Leakage Risk
- **ZERO**, because Battery 85 is in the Training partition, and all offset parameters ($\Delta T$) are fitted exclusively on training cells.

#### 6. Deployment Implication
In deployed fleets, thermocouple detachment or channel failure is a known failure mode. Flagging and imputing temperature ensures that a vehicle or ESS telemetry stream does not cause model crash or output wild health hallucinations upon thermocouple detachment.

### Issue 3: Battery 19 `QCharge` Integration Drift

#### 1. Problem
In **Battery 19**, `QCharge` exhibits an anomalous, linear drift from cycle 12.0 (1.2254 Ah) to cycle 39.0 (1.9679 Ah), increasing at a steady rate of ~+0.0275 Ah per cycle.

#### 2. Evidence
- While `QCharge` rises from 1.225 Ah to 1.9679 Ah, `QDischarge` remains virtually flat at ~1.0698 Ah.
- `SOH` remains completely stable at ~100.04%.
- `chargetime` remains steady at ~9.96 minutes.
- After cycle 39, cycle 40 is skipped (missing), and at cycle 41 `QCharge` immediately resets to 1.0691 Ah.
- This pattern demonstrates classic **coulometric integrator drift** (current shunt sensor bias accumulating in the cycler's integrator until a firmware reset or cycle pause occurs at cycle 40).
- In Task 2I's battery split, **Battery 19 is assigned to the Training partition**.

#### 3. Decision
- **TRANSFORM (Robust Scaling / Train-Fitted Clipping)**
  1. For linear models, neural networks, or distance-based algorithms: clip `QCharge` at the **99.5th percentile computed strictly on the Training partition** ($QCharge_{	ext{clip, upper}} pprox 1.1417	ext{ Ah}$).
  2. For tree-based ensembles (LightGBM, XGBoost, Random Forest): retain the clipped or raw monotonic feature, as decision trees are invariant to monotonic outlier scaling.
  3. **KEEP AS-IS in raw dataset; apply transformation strictly within the ML pipeline feature preprocessor.**

#### 4. Reason
The drift represents a real instrumentation artifact rather than cell swelling or overcharging (since chargetime and discharge capacity were normal). Clipping prevents neural network weight destabilization while preserving the battery's remaining 660 valid cycles.

#### 5. Leakage Risk
- **ZERO**, because the clipping threshold is calculated strictly on the Training partition.

#### 6. Deployment Implication
Deployed BMS current sensors frequently experience drift due to temperature coefficients and zero-offset accumulation. Train-fitted clipping shields downstream models from wild input spikes.

### Issue 4: Extreme Charge Time Resting Spikes (`chargetime > 60 min`)

#### 1. Problem
A total of **17 records** across 11 batteries exhibit `chargetime` values between 278.4 minutes (~4.6 hours) and 3,934.7 minutes (~65.5 hours), compared to the dataset median of 10.31 minutes.

#### 2. Evidence
- 11 of the 17 records cluster tightly at **3,932 to 3,935 minutes (~65.5 hours, exactly 2.7 days)**.
- 3 records cluster at **~419 minutes (~7.0 hours)**.
- 3 records cluster at **~278 to 281 minutes (~4.6 hours)**.
- These discrete clusters correspond precisely to **laboratory hold/pause periods** (e.g., weekend testing holds, maintenance intervals, or test chamber reconditioning) where the cell was left resting under float voltage or open-circuit before the cycler advanced the cycle counter.
- The corresponding capacities (`QDischarge` ~1.05 Ah) and SOH (~98–100%) are completely normal.

#### 3. Decision
- **TRANSFORM (Log-Transform OR Train-Fitted Capping)**
  1. Apply an upper cap to `chargetime` at **30.0 minutes** (or the 99th percentile of the Training partition, $pprox 12.88	ext{ min}$), OR apply a log-transform: $\widetilde{t}_{	ext{charge}} = \log_{10}(1 + 	ext{chargetime})$.
  2. **KEEP AS-IS in raw dataset; apply transformation strictly in feature preprocessing.**

#### 4. Reason
Battery degradation is governed by active electrochemical charge throughput and C-rate, not passive resting time in a laboratory queue. Extreme resting durations distort distance-based and gradient-based algorithms if left unscaled.

#### 5. Leakage Risk
- **ZERO**, as the capping threshold or log-transformation is static or fitted strictly on the Training partition.

#### 6. Deployment Implication
In real-world EV or BESS usage, vehicles may be plugged in over a 3-day holiday weekend. Transforming or capping `chargetime` ensures that the duration of connection does not artificially bias the degradation estimator.

### Issue 5: Transient `Tmin == 0.0 °C` Dropouts

#### 1. Problem
A total of **24 records** contain `Tmin == 0.0 °C`.

#### 2. Evidence
- All 24 occurrences appear in Batteries 87–93.
- In each case, `Tavg` is normal (~30–34 °C) and `Tmax` is normal (~36–39 °C).
- These represent transient single-cycle digital dropouts (ADC channel read zero for one sample in the cycle summary buffer).

#### 3. Decision
- **IMPUTE**
  - Apply **causal forward-fill** ($T_{\min, t} = T_{\min, t-1}$) if the prior cycle has $T_{\min} > 0$.
  - If $t=1$, impute using the battery's subsequent valid cycle or the training partition median offset: $T_{\min} = T_{	ext{avg}} - 	ext{Median}(T_{	ext{avg}} - T_{\min})_{	ext{train}}$.

#### 4. Reason
Single-cycle digital nulls are easily repaired without introducing bias or dropping rows.

#### 5. Leakage Risk
- **ZERO** under causal forward-fill and training-partition fitted offsets.

#### 6. Deployment Implication
Protects telemetry pipelines against single-frame digital bus dropouts.

### Issue 6: Early-Cycle SOH Baseline Exceedance (`SOH > 100%`)

#### 1. Problem
A total of **10,784 records (9.244% of the dataset)** have `SOH > 100.0%`, with values ranging up to `104.79%`.

#### 2. Evidence
- SOH is defined mathematically as:
  $$	ext{SOH}_t = rac{QDischarge_t}{rac{1}{50} \sum_{k=1}^{50} QDischarge_k} 	imes 100$$
- In initial cycles (cycles 1 to ~20), lithium-ion batteries often exhibit capacity break-in, electrolyte wetting, or mild initial temperature variations that cause early discharge capacity to exceed the 50-cycle average.
- 0 records have $	ext{SOH} < 0$, and 0 records have $	ext{SOH} = \infty$.
- The distribution is completely normal and electrochemically expected under this baseline definition.

#### 3. Decision
- **KEEP AS-IS**
  - Retain the exact calculated continuous target values for model training and loss computation.
  - Optional UI/Presentation Layer: When displaying to end-users on dashboards, clip displayed values to $[0\%, 100\%]$ if required by business logic.

#### 4. Reason
Target values represent physical ground-truth capacity ratios. Artificially clipping targets in the ML training dataset would create artificial target gradients at 100% and bias regression loss functions.

#### 5. Leakage Risk
- **ZERO**. SOH is strictly the regression target.

#### 6. Deployment Implication
Ensures the regression model produces calibrated, unbiased predictions around fresh cell states.

### Issue 7: Direct Target Leakage via `QDischarge`

#### 1. Problem
`QDischarge` exhibits a Pearson correlation of **$r = 0.9989$** with the target variable `SOH`.

#### 2. Evidence
- By definition, $	ext{SOH} = (QDischarge / Q_{	ext{baseline}}) 	imes 100$.
- In deployed applications, discharge capacity cannot be measured until an asset undergoes a complete 100% to 0% depth-of-discharge cycle.
- Using `QDischarge` to predict `SOH` represents trivial circular leakage.

#### 3. Decision
- **EXCLUDE FROM PRIMARY MODEL**
  - `QDischarge` is permanently barred from $X_{SOH}$ and $X_{RUL}$ in all primary models.
  - Permitted only in diagnostic benchmark models explicitly marked as "Full Telemetry Benchmark".

#### 4. Reason
Enforces scientific validity and guarantees real-world deployment viability.

#### 5. Leakage Risk
- Eliminates direct target leakage.

#### 6. Deployment Implication
Enables state-of-health estimation from partial charging telemetry without requiring full deep discharges.

### Issue 8: Memorization / Group Key Leakage via `battery_id`

#### 1. Problem
`battery_id` is an arbitrary integer identifier ($1 \le 	ext{battery\_id} \le 139$) assigned in the laboratory.

#### 2. Evidence
- Tree-based models can split on `battery_id` to memorize cell lifespans and degradation curves, yielding artificially perfect validation scores that collapse on unseen batteries.

#### 3. Decision
- **EXCLUDE FROM PRIMARY MODEL**
  - `battery_id` is strictly a metadata grouping key for Cross-Validation and partitioning.
  - It must never be included in feature matrices $X$.

#### 4. Reason
Guarantees generalization to new, unseen battery packs.

#### 5. Leakage Risk
- Eliminates identity memorization leakage.

#### 6. Deployment Implication
Ensures robust model transferability across diverse commercial fleets.

### Issue 9: Ground-Truth `SOH` Leakage into `RUL` Pipeline

#### 1. Problem
`SOH` has a Pearson correlation of **$r = 0.8175$** with `RUL`. Supplying ground-truth `SOH` to the RUL model during production is impossible because true SOH is unknown in the field.

#### 2. Evidence
- Tasks 2G and 2H established the two-stage cascade architecture.

#### 3. Decision
- **EXCLUDE ONLY FROM SPECIFIC MODEL (Production Pipeline)**
  - In production inference, $X_{RUL}$ must consume the **predicted SOH** ($\widehat{	ext{SOH}}$) output by Model 1, never ground-truth `SOH`.
  - During development, an "Oracle RUL Benchmark" may consume ground-truth `SOH` strictly for theoretical ablation.

#### 4. Reason
Reflects operational reality where only charging telemetry is available at inference time.

#### 5. Leakage Risk
- Eliminates cascade ground-truth leakage.

#### 6. Deployment Implication
Seamless, automated joint inference in edge BMS or cloud platforms.

### Issue 10: Cycle Skips and Non-Continuous Sequences

#### 1. Problem
Across the 139 batteries, **8 batteries** have minor cycle skips:
- Batteries 19, 21, 23, 24, 25, 40, 46, and 87 have 1 or 2 missing cycle numbers (total 9 missing cycles across 116,663 records).

#### 2. Evidence
- 131 batteries (94.2%) are 100% continuous ($N_{	ext{records}} = 	ext{max\_cycle} - 	ext{min\_cycle} + 1$).
- The 8 non-continuous batteries have single-cycle gaps (e.g., cycle 40 missing in Battery 19) due to cycler pauses or routine maintenance.

#### 3. Decision
- **KEEP AS-IS**
  - Tabular ML models treat each row independently.
  - If recurrent sequence models (e.g., LSTM, GRU, Transformers) are evaluated in later phases, sequence indexing must handle $\Delta 	ext{cycle}$ explicitly rather than assuming uniform $+1$ steps.

#### 4. Reason
9 missing cycles out of 116,663 (< 0.008%) have negligible impact on tabular modeling.

#### 5. Leakage Risk
- None.

#### 6. Deployment Implication
Telemetry loss of occasional cycles is normal over years of battery service.
